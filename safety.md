In February of this year, a panel of experts from around the globe consisting of nominated representatives from the European Union, the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development, and the United Nations, published the 2026 International AI Safety Report. [https://internationalaisafetyreport.org/publication/international-ai-safety-report-2026] This report evaluates the risks of the most capable general-purpose [definition] AI systems. The report details the rising but uneven capabilities across AI systems, and emerging risks the more they improve.

As these capabilities continue to rise and AI systems gain more autonomy (which is an open goal from many of these companies), if they are not aligned with our goals and morals they will present a clear danger, one that could be uncontrollable if it progresses too far. 

In December of 2025, the Future of Life Institute published the AI Safety index, which assesses different aspects of AI models for the risks they bring to society. No company scored above a C+ overall. These companies currently have huge incentives to continue to cut corners and sacrifice safety in pursuit of raw accelerationism, which puts the entire world at risk. 

This is growing problem as autonomous system utilizing these technologies are given more high-stakes tasks, in which misalignment can become far more pronounced and consequential. the United States' military as identified AI targeting and integration with the kill-chain [definition] as path for development and deployment within the organization [https://media.defense.gov/2026/Jan/12/2003855671/-1/-1/0/ARTIFICIAL-INTELLIGENCE-STRATEGY-FOR-THE-DEPARTMENT-OF-WAR.PDF].
