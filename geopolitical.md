The United States federal government has said that "The United States is in a race to achieve global dominance in artificial intelligence."[^1] [https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf] They have described their strategy as explicitly accelerationist[^2] [https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2025/07/white-house-unveils-americas-ai-action-plan/], prioritizing speed of innovation over safety.

There are legitimate reasons to worry about an opponent's adversarial AI systems; offensive cybersecurity threats are very real, and agentic coding and exploit agents accelerate this problem dramatically. However, every advance in capability presents increasing opportunities for another rogue AI incident [Safety source][Rogue AI source], as well as an giving any opponent's opportunity to reverse engineer our own developments [Open source replication of capability]. We have already seen Chinese models replicate proprietary model capability at a fraction of the cost soon after those capabilities are achieved. The federal government's strategy benefits our defensive security but poses massive detriment to the environment [source], society [source], and our world's safety and security. 

Diplomacy is currently focused on preventing exports to countries we are competing with and responding to incidents themselves, not preventing them. This is nowhere near effective enough; AI experts have warned countless times at the inherent risk developing a model at all has unless heavily safeguarding, which is not a primary focus of the government as of right now [AI safety source].

Addressing the current problems with chatbots, coding agents, and other current implementations of AI, as well as the massive potential future risks [Link to long-term risks], requires a coordinated international push for regulation and cooperation between nations on this issue. 

The most comprehensive framework we have seen addressing AI has been from the EU with their "Artificial Intelligence Act." However, this is also vastly insufficient. This needs to be an international effort that includes the countries that host the most capable model, namely China and the USA. 

We have seen these efforts before; the response to nuclear power was an unprecedented exercise in global cooperation and resolve, addressing one of the most prominent threats of the 20th century. The efforts with not without issue, and still don't function perfectly today, but continue to meaningfully contain the threat of nuclear weapons. 

In August of this year over 1,000 AI frontier employees, asked: "the U.S. government support an international effort to develop the technical and governance tools needed to deliberately pace the frontier of automated AI development."
